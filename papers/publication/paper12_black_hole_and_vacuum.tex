\begin{abstract}

Two questions about a string compactification are routinely run together: \emph{which} $K3$ surface does the
theory pick, and \emph{how many} vacua does it admit. We show, in Lean~4, that the same kind of lattice
arithmetic answers the first uniquely and the second not at all, and we locate exactly what has to be added
before the second can be answered. On the black-hole side, the attractor mechanism on $K3\times T^2$ is made
fully explicit: every integral binary quadratic form is realised by a primitive charge pair; a positive-definite
charge form has discriminant $4ac-b^2\ge 3$, so there is a \emph{smallest} black hole; it has
$p^2=q^2=2$, $p\cdot q=1$, transcendental lattice $A_2$, discriminant $D=-3$ and attractor modulus
$\tau=\omega+1$ with $\omega^3=1$; and its single-centred index is $25353$, computed from a series whose
neighbouring value $-50064$ matches the $50064$ printed by Sen for $Q^2=P^2=2$, $Q\cdot P=0$. Uniqueness, from
arithmetic alone. On the vacuum side, in a $T^6/\Gamma$ orientifold opened as a control, the $O3$ tadpole budget
$N_{D3}+\tfrac12 N_{\rm flux}=16$ has $17$ integer solutions and bounds \emph{no} flux: $H^3(T^6,\mathbb{Z})$ is
unimodular symplectic of rank~$20$, and for every value of the flux contribution infinitely many flux vectors
realise it. The $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$ projection cuts the rank to~$8$ but leaves a unimodular
symplectic lattice, and requiring the quanta to lie in $M\mathbb{Z}$ changes nothing for any $M$, since rescaling
a lattice gives a lattice. What does change it is positivity: the imaginary-self-duality condition replaces the
indefinite pairing by a positive-definite form --- here, forced by $J_8\cdot J_8=-1$, exactly the Euclidean one
--- whose balls are finite, and the infinite family collapses to eleven members at an illustrative ceiling.
Finiteness comes from supersymmetry, never from the budget. We report the programme's experimental status
without softening it: of four derivable observables, three were frozen by git tag with their decision rules
before the verdict, and two are excluded (a $47\,\mu$m extra dimension by torsion-balance and neutron-star
bounds; Hubble-scale holographic dark energy, which forces $q=+1/2$ at every epoch) while two are unreachable and
share a premise already excluded by collider searches. An independent superfluid group refuted the associated
dual-length hypothesis in $^4$He and withdrew its string-theoretic framing. We re-analyse that group's
topological-data-analysis result and find that its measured vortex-line floor, $F=0.943\,\xi$, equals
$\sqrt2\,\Delta x$ to fourteen significant figures with a tenfold degenerate minimum --- a lattice distance, not
yet a physical length. Finally, and methodologically, we report two claims of our own that this apparatus
\emph{failed} to catch and that reading did: a false statement of the crystallographic restriction
($\varphi(n)\le d$, refuted here by four matrices in $SL(6,\mathbb{Z})$ of orders $15,20,24,30$), and a theorem
whose statement was vacuous while its docstring claimed a nerve theorem. Kernel-checked verification certifies
what a proof \emph{depends on}; it is blind to prose nothing depends on and to a statement that says nothing.
All results are checked by the Lean~4 kernel at \texttt{v4.34.0-rc2}; the repository audits $785$ declarations,
$0$ failing, of which $782$ carry mathematical content. The axioms are \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

``Which $K3$?'' and ``how many vacua?'' sound like two instances of one question. They are not, and the
difference is arithmetic rather than philosophical. This paper makes the difference precise by formalizing both
sides in the same language --- integral lattices and their automorphisms, checked by the Lean~4 kernel --- and
observing that they behave in opposite ways.

On the black-hole side the attractor mechanism converts a choice of charge into a choice of surface. The moduli
at the horizon are not free: they are fixed by the charges~\cite{Moore1998}. Once one asks for the \emph{smallest}
black hole with a horizon, the arithmetic returns a single answer, and \cref{sec:bh} derives it from the charge
lattice with no input beyond the definitions.

On the vacuum side the corresponding question is a counting question, and the object usually invoked to make the
count finite is the tadpole budget. \Cref{sec:vac} shows that the budget does no such thing. It bounds one
integer. It never bounds the flux quanta --- not before the orbifold projection, not after it, and not under any
quantisation convention. The lattice that carries the fluxes is unimodular and symplectic, hence indefinite, and
an indefinite form has infinitely many vectors of any given pairing with a fixed partner. That is the whole
mechanism, and no amount of arithmetic removes it.

\begin{headlinebox}
The smallest black hole is unique and computable from the charge lattice: $D=-3$, transcendental lattice $A_2$,
$\tau=\omega+1$. The vacuum count is not determined by the same arithmetic at any stage. Finiteness enters
exactly once, when imaginary self-duality replaces the indefinite pairing by a positive-definite form.
\end{headlinebox}

The hinge is worth stating as a limitation rather than a slogan. The attractor fixes the near-horizon moduli of
\emph{one} black hole. It says nothing about the asymptotic surface, and it leaves the $T^2$ K\"ahler modulus
free~\cite{Moore1998}. So the determinacy of \cref{sec:bh} does not propagate to \cref{sec:vac}; the two sections
are answers to different questions, and the paper's claim is precisely that conflating them is what produces the
appearance of a selection principle where there is none.

Three further sections serve that claim rather than extending it. \Cref{sec:exp} records what the surrounding
research programme has already been made to answer for, because a paper about a programme's mathematics should
state the programme's empirical standing rather than leave a reader to discover it. \Cref{sec:tda} reports the
one empirical datum that bears on a structural ingredient of a current conjecture, together with an independent
re-analysis of it that we performed for this paper and that weakens it. \Cref{sec:method} reports two claims of
our own that the verification apparatus did not catch.

That last section is the one we would ask a sceptical reader to start with. A repository that checks every proof
with a kernel can still carry a false sentence for five tagged releases, and can still count a theorem whose
statement is \texttt{True}. Neither is a failure of the kernel. Both are failures of what the kernel was asked,
and both were caught by reading. We report them because a methodology paper that reports only its successes is
evidence of nothing.

\section{The black hole side: the smallest horizon fixes one surface}\label{sec:bh}

A dyon of the $K3\times T^2$ compactification carries a pair of charges $(p,q)$ valued in the K3 lattice, and at the horizon the attractor mechanism drives the moduli to values determined by $(p,q)$ alone \tierL\ \cite[(4.4a)--(4.6), ll.~1356--1376]{Moore1998}. Moore's reading of this is arithmetic: the charge pair determines a binary quadratic form, the attractive K3 is the surface whose transcendental lattice is $T_S=\langle p,q\rangle$ and whose N\'eron--Severi lattice is $\langle p,q\rangle^{\perp}$ \tierL\ \cite[(4.8), ll.~1404--1409]{Moore1998}, and the $T^2$ acquires a complex structure $\tau(p,q)$. In the formalisation the charges live in a $U+U$ summand of $U^3+E_8(-1)^2=H^2(K3,\mathbb{Z})$, with coordinates $(e_1,f_1,e_2,f_2)$ satisfying $e_i\cdot f_i=1$ and $e_i^2=f_i^2=0$; a form $(a,b,c)$ denotes the Gram matrix $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2a&b\\b&2c\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, of discriminant $b^2-4ac$ \tierA\ \lean{DualScaleDyons/AttractorCharges.lean}.

\begin{headlinebox}
Under the positivity that supergravity imposes on the charge form \tierL\ \cite[(3.4)--(3.5), ll.~1104--1112]{Moore1998}, the arithmetic has a floor, $D\le-3$ \tierA\ \lean{discriminant\_gap}, and the floor is attained by the single reduced class $(1,1,1)$, with $T_S=A_2$ \tierA\ \lean{smallest\_black\_hole} and $\tau=(1+i\sqrt3)/2$ \tierA\ \lean{tau\_minimal}. On this side of the paper the answer is unique.
\end{headlinebox}


\begin{remark}[Two labels, one torus]
\label{rem:tau}
The kernel statement gives $\tau=\omega+1$ \tierA\ \lean{tau\_minimal}, while the reading below, and Moore's
discussion, speak of the $T^2$ ``at $\tau=\omega$''. The two are the same object and not merely isomorphic ones:
$\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}(\omega+1)=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\omega$ as subsets of $\mathbb{C}$, since each generator of
either lattice is an integral combination of the generators of the other. Equivalently, $\tau\mapsto\tau+1$ is
the modular transformation $T=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\in SL(2,\mathbb{Z})$. We
keep the label $\omega+1$ wherever a theorem is quoted and $\omega$ wherever the geometry is described, and note
the identification here once rather than silently switching between them.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Every form is a charge, primitively.} The first link is a realisation statement. For all integers $a,b,c$, the charges $p=e_1+a\,f_1$ and $q=e_2+b\,f_1+c\,f_2$ have Gram matrix $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2a&b\\b&2c\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, and they span a \emph{primitive} sublattice: every integral vector in their rational span is an integer combination of $p$ and $q$ \tierA\ \lean{every\_form\_is\_charged}. This is Moore's realisation claim, and it is proved for every form rather than checked on a sample. Primitivity is not decoration: it is exactly the hypothesis under which Moore's classification applies, namely that for primitive $\langle p,q\rangle$ two charge pairs are U-dual if and only if their forms are $SL(2,\mathbb{Z})$-equivalent, by Nikulin's uniqueness of the primitive embedding \tierL\ \cite[(3.11), ll.~1178--1190]{Moore1998}. Taken together: every form occurs as a primitive charge pair \tierA, and on such pairs U-duality acts as $SL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ acts on forms \tierL.

\paragraph{The arithmetic has a floor.} Moore's charge form is $Q_{p,q}=\tfrac12\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}p^2&-p\cdot q\\-p\cdot q&q^2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, with $D=(p\cdot q)^2-p^2q^2$, and supergravity requires $p^2>0$ and $Q_{p,q}>0$ \tierL\ \cite[(3.4)--(3.5), ll.~1104--1112]{Moore1998}. The formalised results carry the arithmetic form of that condition, $0<4ac-b^2$, as an explicit hypothesis.

\begin{proposition}[\tierA\ \lean{discriminant\_gap}]
For integers $a,b,c$ with $0<4ac-b^2$ one has $3\le 4ac-b^2$, and $4ac-b^2=3$ forces $b$ odd. Equivalently, the discriminant satisfies $D\le-3$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent Read through the supergravity condition \tierL\ \cite[(3.4)--(3.5), ll.~1104--1112]{Moore1998}, the gap says that charge pairs with a horizon have $D\le-3$: there is a smallest black hole. For context, the number of U-duality orbits at fixed $D$ is $N(D)=\sum_m h(D/m^2)$ \tierL\ \cite[(3.17)--(3.18), ll.~1250--1262]{Moore1998}; we do not use it to establish uniqueness.

\begin{theorem}[\tierA\ \lean{smallest\_black\_hole}]
The bound is saturated by $p^2=q^2=2$, $p\cdot q=1$, giving $T_S=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)=A_2$ and $D=-3$. The reduced class is $(1,1,1)$ and it is the only one; primitivity is confirmed for this pair, and $\mathrm{reduce}(1,-1,1)=(1,1,1)$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent The last equality is bookkeeping between two labels this section needs. Moore's $Q_{p,q}$ is half the Gram matrix with the off-diagonal sign reversed \tierL\ \cite[(3.4)--(3.5), ll.~1104--1112]{Moore1998}, and the attractor variety is stated in terms of the doubled form, $S_{2Q}\times E_{\tau(p,q)}$ \tierL\ \cite[(4.27)--(4.29), ll.~1580--1600]{Moore1998}; at the floor the two labels are $(1,-1,1)$ and $(1,1,1)$, and reduction identifies them \tierA\ \lean{smallest\_black\_hole}. Moore also records that the abelian surfaces $A_Q=E_{\tau_1}\times E_{\tau_2}$ with $\rho=4$ correspond one-to-one to $PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ classes of $Q$, by Shioda--Mitani \tierL\ \cite[(4.15), ll.~1480--1507]{Moore1998}.

\paragraph{The attractor then fixes the torus.} For every charge with $D<0$, $\tau(p,q)=\bigl(p\cdot q+i\sqrt{-D}\bigr)/p^2$ solves Moore's equation $p^2\tau^2-2(p\cdot q)\tau+q^2=0$ and lies in the upper half plane \tierA\ \lean{attractor\_tau}, matching $\tau=(p\cdot q+\sqrt D)/p^2$ \tierL\ \cite[(4.4a)--(4.6), ll.~1356--1376]{Moore1998}. At the floor this gives $\tau=(1+i\sqrt3)/2=\omega+1$ with $\omega^3=1$, $\omega\neq1$; at $D=-4$, that is $p\cdot q=0$, it gives $\tau=i$ \tierA\ \lean{tau\_minimal}.

\paragraph{Degeneracies, and one external anchor.} The Fourier coefficients of $\psi_m^F$ count single-centred black holes \tierL\ \cite[(1.6), ll.~426--443]{DMZ2012}. Computed from the Stream~5 series through $q^3$ and $1/\eta^{24}$, the coefficient $c$ of $\psi_1^F$ at $(n,l)=(1,1)$, i.e.\ at charges $(q^2/2,\,p\cdot q,\,p^2/2)=(1,1,1)$, is $25353$, and at $(1,0)$ it is $-50064$ \tierA\ \lean{smallest\_black\_hole\_index}. Sen prints $d(Q,P)=50064$ for $Q^2=P^2=2$, $Q\cdot P=0$, for the heterotic string on $T^6$ \tierL\ \cite[l.~6788]{Sen2007}. That entry is the $D=-4$ black hole, and matching it fixes the sign convention $d=(-1)^{l+1}c$. In that convention the $D=-3$ black hole has $d=25353$: the same kernel-checked series one step over. It does not appear in Sen's table, and we claim no more for it than that.

\paragraph{A companion negative control.} The single-centred (``immortal'') dyon counting function at $m=1$ satisfies $\Delta\psi_1^F=3E_4A-648H$ through $q^3$, with $H$ the Hurwitz--Kronecker class numbers obtained by counting reduced forms rather than transcribed \tierA\ \lean{immortal\_m1}, \lean{hurwitz\_values} in \lean{DualScaleDyons/Immortal.lean}. The same identity without the class-number term is false \tierA\ \lean{immortal\_m1\_needs\_H}, so the theorem set carries its own check that the arithmetic input is doing work.

\paragraph{The reading.} \tierC\ The smallest black hole with a horizon has near-horizon geometry $X_3\times E_\omega$: the Kummer-type K3 whose transcendental lattice is $A_2$, with the $T^2$ at $\tau=\omega$. This breaks an earlier $i/\omega$ tie \emph{for black holes}.

\begin{scopebox}
The smallest black hole selects $\omega$. The theory does not. It says nothing about the vacuum: the attractor fixes the near-horizon moduli of one black hole, not the asymptotic K3, and it leaves the $T^2$ K\"ahler modulus free --- the attractor variety is $88$-dimensional \tierL\ \cite[(4.3), ll.~1351--1353]{Moore1998}. The repository's standing conclusion is that there is no single preferred K3, and that the question is ill-posed until one says which physics is doing the selecting. Here the selector is named: one charge configuration, at its own horizon.
\end{scopebox}

Uniqueness on this side is therefore a statement about a selector, not about the theory; the vacuum side, treated next, supplies a different selector and gets infinitely many answers.

\section{The vacuum side: the same arithmetic decides nothing}\label{sec:vac}

This section runs lattice arithmetic of the same kind used on the black-hole side of this paper over a different
compactification, and finds that it fixes nothing: the number of integer solutions is infinite, and stays
infinite under the one strengthening of the integrality hypothesis formalised here, namely an overall
normalisation factor. Finiteness appears once a positivity condition is added by hand. \tierC\ That positivity
is the \emph{only} ingredient with that effect, and that the contrast says anything about physical vacua, are
readings and not theorems. The $T^6/\Gamma$ orientifold is opened here as a \emph{control}: it is there to show
what kind of input fixes an answer. It is not a continuation of the dual-scale hypothesis, and no conclusion
drawn here transfers to $K3 \times T^2$. All Lean files named below sit under \lean{DualScaleStream2/}.

\subsection{The lattice and the parity anti-isometry}

\tierA\ In \lean{Orientifold/NarainT6.lean} the Narain lattice of $T^6$ is built as $\Gamma_{6,6} = U^{6}$: the
Gram matrix is symmetric with even diagonal, unimodular via an exhibited integer inverse, and of signature
$(6,6)$, certified by six explicit vectors of norm $+2$ and six of norm $-2$.

\tierA\ \lean{parity\_antiisometry} records the structural point that governs everything downstream: worldsheet
parity $\Omega$ satisfies $\Omega^{T} G\, \Omega = -G$. It is an \emph{anti}-isometry, because exchanging left-
and right-movers reverses the sign of $p_L^2 - p_R^2$. Consequently an orientifold group is not a subgroup of
$O(6,6;\mathbb{Z})$; it is generated by the isometries $\theta_i$ together with one element that negates the form.

\tierA\ The general consequence is isolated in \lean{Orientifold/InvariantSublattice.lean} as
\lean{antiisometry\_fixed\_isotropic}: if $P^{T} G P = -G$ and $Pv = v$ then $Q(v) = 0$. Applied to
$P = \Omega\theta_1$, the fixed sublattice is spanned by six explicit vectors and all $36$ pairings among them
vanish --- the sublattice is totally isotropic, not merely null on the diagonal --- and rank $6$ is maximal for
signature $(6,6)$.

\tierA\ \lean{plane\_charge\_total} closes the charge bookkeeping: $2^{9-p}\cdot(-2^{p-5}) = -16$ for
$p = 3,5,7,9$, so for $p = 3$ there are $64$ planes of charge $-1/4$. \tierL\ As a cross-check in the O7 frame,
and for nothing else, we record the statement $4\cdot(-4) + 16\cdot 1 = 0$ of \cite{Sen1996}, ll.~238--250.

\subsection{A budget that looks finite, in the wrong variable}

\tierA\ \lean{Flux/T6TadpoleFiniteness.lean} shows that the budget $N_{D3} + \tfrac{1}{2}N_{\mathrm{flux}} = 16$
has exactly $17$ integer solutions, $\{(16-k,k) : k \le 16\}$. The file states the caveat itself, and we repeat
it: these are not vacua. One value of $\tfrac12 N_{\mathrm{flux}}$ corresponds to many flux quanta, and once
anti-branes are admitted there are infinitely many integer solutions. \tierC\ Read against the theorems that
follow, the apparent finiteness sits in the budget variable rather than in the data a vacuum would be built from.

\subsection{The flux lattice: integrality gives infinity}

\tierA\ \lean{Flux/FluxLattice.lean} turns that caveat into a theorem. The rank of $H^3(T^6,\mathbb{Z})$ is
$\binom{6}{3} = 20$; its intersection form $J$ satisfies $J\!\cdot\!J = -1$ and $J^{T} = -J$, hence is
unimodular. Then \lean{flux\_pairing\_family} and \lean{flux\_family\_injective}: for every $k$, the family
$F(m) = k f_0 + m e_1$ has $\langle e_0, F(m)\rangle = k$ for \emph{every} $m$, injectively in $m$. For every
value of the flux contribution there are infinitely many flux vectors realising it.

\tierA\ \lean{Flux/FluxQuantisation.lean} shows that this survives a rescaling of the quantum. Parametrically in
a normalisation factor $M$: if every quantum of $H$ and $F$ lies in $M\mathbb{Z}$ then
$\langle H,F\rangle \in M^2\mathbb{Z}$ (\lean{pairing\_dvd}); every multiple of $M^2$ is attained
(\lean{pairing\_attains}); and for every $M$ and every attainable value, infinitely many quantised pairs realise
it. Rescaling a lattice gives a lattice.

\tierL\ \cite{GKP2001}, ll.~534--547, eq.~(2.25), requires $(1/2\pi\alpha')\int_C F_3 \in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$ and
likewise for $H_3$, over every 3-cycle $C$. That is period integrality. \tierC\ On our reading, the hypothesis
formalised above is the lattice counterpart of that condition and asserts no more than it does.

\subsection{Projecting changes the rank, not the conclusion}

\tierA\ \lean{Flux/InvariantH3.lean} implements the $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$ with $\theta_1 = -1$ on
$x_1,\dots,x_4$ and $\theta_2 = -1$ on $x_3,\dots,x_6$. A basis 3-form $dx_S$ is invariant iff
$|S \cap \{1,2,3,4\}|$ and $|S \cap \{3,4,5,6\}|$ are both even; the invariant triples are exactly the $8$ with
one index from each 2-torus. Rank $20 \to 8$ is a real reduction. The set is closed under complement, so the
wedge pairing restricts, and \lean{symJ8\_is\_wedge} verifies all $64$ entries of the restricted $8\times 8$
matrix --- the zeros included --- against the wedge sign of the corresponding pair of triples; the literal is
computed in the kernel, not asserted. $J_8^{T} = -J_8$, $J_8\!\cdot\!J_8 = -1$, unimodular. The invariant
sublattice is again a unimodular symplectic lattice, and the infinite family runs inside it.

\subsection{Positivity is what cuts it down}

\tierA\ \lean{Flux/ISDFiniteness.lean}. Because $J_8\!\cdot\!J_8 = -1$, the map $J_c = -J_8$ is a complex
structure, $J_c^2 = -1$, compatible with the symplectic form, $J_c^{T} J_8 J_c = J_8$. The associated form
$g(v,w) = \omega(v, J_c w)$, where $\omega$ is the symplectic form of $J_8$, is then, by \lean{gForm\_eq\_dot},
exactly the Euclidean dot product on $\mathbb{Z}^8$ --- forced by $J_8\!\cdot\!J_8 = -1$, not chosen. It is
positive definite, and every ball $\{v : g(v,v) \le B\}$ is finite, with the explicit coordinate bound
$v_i^2 \le B$. The family satisfies $g(F(m),F(m)) = k^2 + m^2$, so at the ceiling $32$ with $k = 1$ it is cut to
exactly eleven members, at an illustrative ceiling ($-5 \le m \le 5$).

\tierL\ \cite{GKP2001} supplies the physical precedent for adding a positivity condition, not the source of $g$:
the imaginary self-duality condition $*_6 G_3 = i G_3$ (ll.~629--631, eq.~(2.31)) and the decomposition
$G = G^{+} + G^{-}$ with $*_6 G^{\pm} = \mp\, i G^{\pm}$ (ll.~1801--1812, eq.~(A.13)), in which the flux action

\section{Method, and a claim of ours that the method refuted}\label{sec:method}

The apparatus is described at the end of the section; a failure introduces it
better than a design does. The most instructive event of these releases was not a
theorem proved but a sentence this project had written down, carried through five
tagged releases and every gate, and found false as soon as someone tried to prove
it.

\begin{correctionbox}
\textbf{The claim we made, and retract.} For five releases a file header and two
documentation paragraphs stated the crystallographic restriction in the form: a
finite-order integer matrix of size $d$ and order $n$ exists only if
$\varphi(n) \le d$, with $\varphi$ Euler's totient. It was listed as
``the next formalization target''. On attempting the proof it turned out to be
\emph{false}. \tierA{} The counterexamples that are kernel-checked are at $d = 6$;
\tierC{} the corresponding failure for every $d \ge 5$ is asserted here and is not
formalized.
\end{correctionbox}

\subsection*{Where the standard argument breaks}

The tempting route looks safe. If $A^n = 1$ then the minimal
polynomial of $A$ divides $X^n - 1 = \prod_{e \mid n} \Phi_e$; some eigenvalue must
be a \emph{primitive} $n$-th root of unity; hence $\Phi_n$ divides the characteristic
polynomial, and $\deg \Phi_n = \varphi(n) \le d$. The middle step is false. The order
of a matrix is the \emph{least common multiple} of the orders of its eigenvalues, not
the largest of them. The matrix $\mathrm{diag}(C_{\Phi_3}, C_{\Phi_5})$, built from the companion
matrices of $\Phi_3$ and $\Phi_5$, has order
$\mathrm{lcm}(3,5) = 15$ and has no eigenvalue of order $15$ at all: every eigenvalue
has order $3$ or $5$. Nothing forces $\Phi_n$ into the characteristic polynomial.

\subsection*{The methodological point}

Two instances make a general form. An automated apparatus certifies what a proof
\emph{depends on}. It is therefore structurally blind to (i) prose that nothing
depends on, including prose naming the project's own next goal, and (ii) a theorem
whose statement is vacuous, or weaker than its docstring. Neither is a gap in the
kernel; both are gaps in what the kernel was asked. No theorem depended on the false
crystallographic statement, so the build passed, the \texttt{sorry}-grep passed, the
axiom audit passed and the statement lock passed, through five tagged releases, with
the false sentence in place the whole time. What caught it was attempting the proof.
The rule adopted: a statement written as ``not proved here; the next target is $X$''
is an unverified \emph{claim}, and must be checked on a small case before it is
written down at all.

\subsection*{The apparatus}

Lean 4 v4.34.0-rc2 with Mathlib at the same revision. Five gates precede any claim
that something is proved: build the library target; grep for \texttt{sorry},
\texttt{admit} and \texttt{native\_decide}; the axiom audit
\lean{tools/axiom\_audit.py}; the statement lock \lean{tools/statement\_lock.py --check};
and independent recompilation of any delegated output.

\tierA{} The current state is $785$ theorems audited across ten first-party
libraries, $0$ failing (\lean{tools/axiom\_audit.py}): DualScaleStream2 $176$,
DualScaleDyons $115$, DualScaleMoonshine $101$, StringTheoryFormalization $89$,
StringTheoryFoundation $63$, DualScaleM24Formalization $62$, DualScaleCosmology $59$,
Lean5Corpus $53$, DoubleFieldTheory $44$, DualScaleValidation $23$. The statement
lock covers $1135$ declarations across $102$ files
(\lean{tools/statement\_lock.py --check}). ``$0$ failing'' means that every theorem
depends on nothing beyond the three axioms \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}.

Negative controls run on every release: a deliberately wrong variant of a theorem
must fail to compile. From the releases reported here --- a non-invariant triple
added to an invariant list; a sign flipped in the $8 \times 8$ pairing matrix; a zero
entry of that matrix made nonzero; the family norm $k^2 + m^2$ weakened to
$k^2 + m$; the cut $|m| \le 5$ widened to $|m| \le 6$; $\mathrm{mat}_{15}^{15}$
weakened to $\mathrm{mat}_{15}^{14}$; the order $15$ dropped from the corrected
crystallographic list --- all were caught. Mutation testing, however, catches a
\emph{wrong} theorem and not a \emph{vacuous} one: the mutants of a vacuous statement
are frequently still true. Both instances above had to be caught by reading, not by
a gate.

One provenance rule learned in these releases: a hand-computed matrix literal is not
kernel-verified content until a lemma derives every entry, zeros included, from the
definition. \tierA{} The $8 \times 8$ pairing matrix of the vacuum section is covered
by \lean{symJ8\_is\_wedge}, which checks all $64$ entries against the wedge sign.
Without it the kernel would be certifying internal consistency, not provenance.

\section{Conclusion, and what would move it}
\label{sec:concl}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection*{The separation}

The same arithmetic, asked two questions, behaves in two ways. Asked for the smallest black hole with a horizon,
it returns one surface, one discriminant and one modulus, and pins the answer to an externally printed
degeneracy. Asked how many flux configurations a budget permits, it returns infinitely many, and keeps returning
infinitely many after every refinement one might hope would help: the orbifold projection, which genuinely cuts
the rank from $20$ to $8$ but leaves the lattice unimodular and symplectic; and the quantisation convention,
which for any factor $M$ rescales a lattice into a lattice.

The asymmetry has a one-line cause. The attractor problem is posed on a \emph{definite} form --- a charge form
must be positive definite for a horizon to exist, and definiteness is what makes ``smallest'' meaningful and
makes the minimiser unique. The flux problem is posed on an \emph{indefinite} one, and an indefinite unimodular
lattice has no smallest anything. When imaginary self-duality is imposed, it supplies precisely the missing
definiteness, and the count becomes finite immediately. Finiteness in flux compactifications is a consequence of
supersymmetry, not of topology; \cref{sec:vac} is a formal statement of how little topology contributes.

\subsection*{What is not settled, stated as work}

\begin{scopebox}
\textbf{(i) The normalisation bridge.} Nothing in this paper connects the lattice pairing $\langle H,F\rangle$ to
the physical $N_{\rm flux}$. That needs the $D3$-charge normalisation on the quotient, from a source. Until it is
pinned, the ceiling $32$ is carried over and the count \emph{eleven} is an illustration of a mechanism, not a
number about vacua. \textbf{(ii) The crystallographic restriction} in its correct form $\psi(n)\le d$ remains
unproved here; only its failure in the $\varphi$ form, and the realisability of the four extra orders, are
established. \textbf{(iii) Which complex structure.} The positive-definite form used in \cref{sec:vac} comes from
the canonical compatible complex structure the lattice already carries; the physical one is fixed by moduli. The
mechanism is choice-independent, the count is not. \textbf{(iv) No vacua are counted anywhere.} No moduli
stabilisation, no equations of motion, no quotient by the duality group. \textbf{(v) The TDA floor} sits at a
lattice distance to fourteen significant figures; a run at finer resolution is required before it can be read as
a physical length.
\end{scopebox}

\subsection*{What would move it}

Three things, in increasing order of difficulty. First, a pinned source for the $D3$-charge normalisation on the
quotient: that single input converts the mechanism of \cref{sec:vac} into a statement with a number in it, and it
is a matter of finding the convention, not of proving anything. Second, the imaginary-self-duality condition
imposed as a condition on the rank-$8$ lattice rather than represented by a compatible complex structure chosen
for convenience. Third, a superfluid dataset with $\xi/\Delta x\gtrsim5$, which is the only thing that can
distinguish a healing-length floor from a grid constant.

\subsection*{A remark on method}

We have reported two defects of our own: a false claim about the crystallographic restriction that survived five
tagged releases, and a theorem whose statement was vacuous under a header reading \texttt{VERIFIED}. Both passed
every gate, because every gate is a dependency check. The corollary for anyone reading a formalization headline
is worth stating plainly: a count of ``theorems verified'' counts declarations whose axiom dependencies were
checked. It does not count mathematical content, it does not audit statements against their documentation, and it
cannot distinguish a deep theorem from \texttt{True}. In this repository the difference is currently three
declarations out of $785$; we say so because the number is only useful if what it excludes is stated.
